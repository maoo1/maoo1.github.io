# Heart Heart

This project was an introduction to circuitry, milling my first PCB into the shape of a heart that has a light up LED

[![Watch demo](/blog/heart-heart/lit_up_heart.jpg)](https://youtube.com/shorts/Ew-RXpFyaw4)

## Process

During the beginning of the process, I didn't really know what I was doing. I really liked the idea of doing a heart because I wanted to gift it to my partner for Valentine's Day. My partner is not very techy, so I thought this would be a good gift for not only showing my appreciation for them but also sharing my work. 

However, I didn't want to stop at just a simple heart outline, I wanted the entire circuit to embody the heart. So, when orienting the shape of my circuit, I intentionally tried to make the wiring also resemble the heart -- I tried to maintain symmetry whenever possible, placed the resistors 1 and 2 at slight angles to mirror the curves, and the battery at the bottom to symbolize the point. Additionally, with the wiring, I tried to make a heart outline. But because the spacing was a little tricky for the arrangement I was going for, instead of one cohesive heart shape, I tried to make it look like layer of hearts, with the innermost wires running in parallel with the outermost wires. 

![Wire Shape](/blog/heart-heart/finished_milling.jpg)

Additionally, in terms of choosing the values for the parts, I decided to have capacitor 1 be a larger value, allowing for the LED to be flashing at such a speed it functionally never blinked, staying on forever. I liked option a lot more than a blinking LED because it made the light the center of attention. I was hoping people couldn't help be drawn to the center, and then have their eyes shift outwards to take in the rest of the shapes, slowly realizing it's a heart within a heart within a heart, ect. 

## Reflections

Overall, I'm extremely happy with this project. Prior, the skills of hardware seemed really inaccessible to me. There was always complicated jargon and a ladder of skills that intimidated me from learning hardware, but this project felt good. Although it certaintly wasn't the easiest, I found it really enjoyable to use my skills to create a product of love.
